# Chart Fanatics Trading Strategies: Structured Rule Sheets

This document contains the structured trading rule sheets extracted from three specific Chart Fanatics YouTube videos. Each rule sheet focuses on concrete, codifiable rules, avoiding pure motivational content or "vibes."

---

## Rule Sheet 1: Anthony Crudele's Bollinger Band Strategy
**Video Title:** "If You Only Watch One Trading Strategy Video, Make It This" (Featuring Anthony Crudele)
**URL:** https://youtu.be/EZ_L7zovyrw

### 1. Instrument(s) and Session/Time Windows
Anthony Crudele focuses his trading on the major stock indexes, specifically the S&P 500 (ES), Nasdaq (NQ), and Russell 2000. He utilizes both futures contracts and options on these futures.

He operates primarily as a swing trader, holding positions for "one day to let's call it five days." His analysis strictly begins on the daily chart. As he states, "Everything's a daily for me... the daily sets the tone." After identifying the macro environment on the daily chart, he drops down to lower timeframes (such as the 60-minute, 30-minute, or 3-minute) for specific execution. He does not specify particular times of day for entries.

### 2. Exact Entry Setup
His strategy relies heavily on a 20-period, 3-standard deviation Bollinger Band to identify market environments. He pairs this with a custom indicator called "Beacon," which acts as an automatic Fibonacci retracement drawn from the peak of the Bollinger Band, with key levels at 100% (peak), 70%, 50%, and 30%. He states, "So instead of using a fib on price, I use a fib on volatility."

He identifies three specific market environments and trades them accordingly:

**Expansion (Trend Following) Entry:**
He looks for the Bollinger Bands to point outward, signaling range expansion. 
> "Anytime I see this happen. We get this Bollinger band starts breaking uh to the upside, this one's to the downside, expansion. Make a higher high, right, and this Bollinger band starts going that way and the market takes out these previous highs, I'm long only."

**Mean Reversion Entry:**
He looks for the Bollinger Band to make a peak and stop expanding. He then waits for price to retrace and close below the 30% Beacon line.
> "What I look for is once we get a daily close below 30%... from these two peaks, my target is 50%. So now I will buy puts or I will short futures until a daily closes above there."

**Consolidation (2-Way Tape) Rule:**
When the bands contract and price is ranging, his rule is to "trade on the edges of the range, stay out of the middle."

### 3. Stop Placement Rule
Crudele prefers to use options rather than futures to define his risk, especially in highly volatile expansion phases where futures stops would need to be uncomfortably wide.

For futures, a theoretical stop would be placed below a recent swing low. However, he often avoids this if the risk/reward is poor, stating, "Well that risk reward is is is crap. I can't trade it that way. I wouldn't trade it that way."

Instead, he prefers options: "I'll buy out of the money puts in that situation uh because for me at that moment I can um just pay for the put and basically put it in my back pocket and say however much I'm willing to risk, I'm willing to risk."

For Mean Reversion trades, his stop rule is to stay in the short trade "until a daily closes above there [the 30% line]."

### 4. Target Rule and Trade Management
His targeting rules depend on the market environment identified by his setup.

For Mean Reversion trades, his target is strict: "my target is 50%... This is my target once it hits 50% I'm done."

He also utilizes an "Unfinished Business" target. If a Bollinger Band makes a peak and price reverses before hitting the band again, that old peak becomes a target if the trend resumes. "If the market were to continue to go higher in this moment as it trends up, that previous peak becomes a target."

For Expansion targets, he relies on lower timeframes and external analysis: "I'll go down to a 60-minute chart and it'll help me with targets. I'll also use other people you know for targets like bigger picture targets like my friend Pax who I know what his bigger picture targets are, so I'll look and see what those are and I'll do something like that." He also anchors a VWAP to the high and uses 1, 2, and 3 standard deviations lower as exit guides.

### 5. Risk per Trade or Sizing Rule
Risk is always defined as a fixed fiat dollar amount, not a static number of contracts. His position size fluctuates based on the required stop distance.
> "My amount that I'm willing to risk, my risk capital is always going to be a dollar amount and then my position size is going to be adjusted towards that. It's always about the distance of risk. So if my distance of risk one day is 20 points and the next day it's 200, I can't have, you know, 10 E-minis on for a 200 point risk versus where I should have 50 on for a 2 point risk."

### 6. Filters & Avoidance Rules
His primary filter is the **Macro Environment**. He builds a "macro theme" based on factors like tariffs, gold prices, bond volatility, and the strength of the dollar. If his technical signal goes against his macro theme, he filters the trade by either reducing his size drastically or using options further out in time to withstand the expected volatility.

### 7. Discretionary / Feel-Based Elements ⚠️
Crudele explicitly states, "I use discretion in mine." This discretion is heavily applied to position sizing and instrument selection (choosing between futures or options) based on how well the technical setup aligns with his macro backdrop.
> "If I'm going against what I think the overall macro theme is, I have to be small enough um to be able to withstand a little shimmy shake to be able to maybe let that trade work out."

### 8. Specific Live Trades Shown
No specific, executed live trades (with exact dates, entry prices, stop prices, and PnL outcomes) are detailed in this segment. The speaker uses a historical chart of the "June contract for the E-mini S&P" to visually demonstrate the concepts of consolidation, expansion, and mean reversion.

## Rule Sheet 2: Steven Dux's Short Selling Strategies
**Video Title:** "If You Only Watch One Trading Strategy Video, Make It This" (Featuring Steven Dux)
**URL:** https://youtu.be/52ZsDmFHqyY

### 1. Instrument(s) and Session/Time Windows
Steven Dux trades small-cap stocks. He focuses heavily on the morning session but avoids the immediate open. He states, "Wait until 11:00, start a partial position." He also notes that "after 11:30... volume dies out."

### 2. Exact Entry Setup
Dux outlines three specific short-selling patterns. Before applying the pattern rules, a stock must pass his strict general filtering criteria:
*   **Initial Market Cap:** "The initial market cap has to be between 1 to 100 million... If the market cap is over 200 million, then it's not doable."
*   **Float:** "The float has to be anywhere between 1 million to 50 million... if the float is over 50 million, majority of the time, 99% of the time it's not really tradable."
*   **Price:** "The price has to be over $3. If it's not over $3, it becomes very difficult."

**Setup 1: Gap Up Short (GS)**
1.  Stock "gaps over 100%, 70%, sometimes even 1000%" in the morning.
2.  Check Pre-market volume. "If the pre-market trades over 50 million... that means after market open, we're going to trade more volume... the typical ratio is around 1 to 5 to 1 to 10... the entire day volume trades around 500 million... it's gonna be really crowded... very difficult for a short strategy to work."
3.  Wait for the morning volume rush to subside (concentrated between 9:30 and 11:30).
4.  Look for "mass consolidations" after the initial push.
5.  **Entry:** "Wait until 11:00, start a partial position. And after the momentum shifts... maybe cracks about 5% to 3%, size in full position."

**Setup 2: Bounce Short (BS)**
1.  Identify a historical spike. "After the big spike into five from maybe one dollar, it drops all the way down and stays flat for two months ideally."
2.  Calculate the historical "Dollar Block": "We use the bottom 30 million [volume] times the price... the most consolidated price. So... 30 million times 5... you're looking at maybe 150 million dollar block."
3.  A new "gap up short" occurs on the same ticker.
4.  Estimate the current day's dollar volume based on pre-market volume.
5.  Compare current estimated dollar volume to the historical dollar block. The setup is only valid if the current volume is much less than the historical volume. "The volume ratio becomes... 2 to 1... 1 to 1... 10 to 1... that significantly increases the bounce short winning percentage."
6.  **Entry:** "Wait until 11, start a partial position, and after the momentum shifts... cracks about 5% to 3%, size in full position."

**Setup 3: First Red Day (FRD)**
1.  Look for "parabolic action... the price trend is speeding up, and it's trading more volume on the previous day."
2.  Must have "consecutive green days with higher volume... at least three consecutive green days."
3.  Range requirement: "The range creates for three days minimum has to be 300%... for two days has to be 1000%."
4.  The volume on the potential red day must be lower. "The current day volume estimate has to trade much lower compared to the concentrated volume that traded on the first green day."
5.  **Entry:** "You spot the first red day and you short on the second day." (Meaning the day *after* the first red day).

### 3. Stop Placement Rule
His stop placement is based on the consolidation pattern formed before the entry.
> "Your stop loss will be above the consolidation."

If there was a spike before the consolidation, he maintains the stop around the consolidation area rather than the absolute peak of the spike.

### 4. Target Rule and Trade Management
Dux relies on statistical averages for how much a setup typically fades rather than rigid R:R ratios.
*   **Gap Up Short:** "The average fading percentage... is around 26%."
*   **First Red Day:** "It fades about 75% from the very top."

For trade management, he initiates a "partial position" and then adds to a "full position" once the "momentum shifts" and the stock "cracks about 5% to 3%". He aims to "ride your selling pressure all the way down."

### 5. Risk per Trade or Sizing Rule
He has a hard limit on position sizing relative to the stock's liquidity to avoid moving the market.
> "Ideally you do not want to exceed 10% of the float... or 1% of the volume... If you size in too much, especially on a low float, you are likely to break the supply and demand chain."

He dynamically adjusts his size based on the quality of the setup (specifically the volume ratios in the Bounce Short). If the setup is less ideal, he will "only size in maybe one-fourth of your positions." If the setup is ideal, "the higher the ratio is, the more I will size in."

### 6. Filters & Avoidance Rules
*   **Sectors:** He strictly avoids Biotech, Energy, and Chinese stocks. "There's three sectors... you cannot trade. First of all is Biotech... the winning percentage drops by about 20 to 30%... The second one is the energy sector... The third... Chinese stocks... if you trade less than 30 million [volume], I have seen tickers get halted and it's really thin."
*   **Over-extension:** "If it's really overextended, let's say something goes up to from 1 to 1000, now maybe that's something to consider [avoiding]."
*   **Pre-market Volume:** He filters out Gap Up Shorts if the pre-market volume is too high (e.g., over 50 million), as it indicates the trade will be too crowded.

### 7. Discretionary / Feel-Based Elements ⚠️
While he emphasizes a statistics-based approach to "eliminate your emotion from being FOMO," he acknowledges the role of human psychology in trading, specifically in anticipating when trapped traders will sell to break even (creating "massive selling pressure... just based on human psychology").

### 8. Specific Live Trades Shown
The video uses historical chart replays to illustrate the patterns, rather than showing live, real-time trade executions with P&L. He discusses examples like SLV (First Red Day), BIRD (a failed Gap Up Short due to high volume), EEIQ (Bounce Short), and ASTC (Bounce Short).

## Rule Sheet 3: Okala's 80/20 Nasdaq Strategy
**Video Title:** "80/20 Nasdaq Strategy" (Featuring Okala)
**URL:** https://youtu.be/jsUTbjwpFVk

### 1. Instrument(s) and Session/Time Windows
Okala exclusively trades the Nasdaq (NQ). He states, "these levels I over the years they have worked only on the Nasdaq."

He trades the New York Open, stating, "The edge is in volatility." He explicitly avoids the lunch hour and advises to "do your best to stay out of slow, choppy conditions or slow trending conditions."

He uses two timeframes in conjunction:
*   **10-minute chart:** Used for the "overall sort of bigger picture" and marking out structure.
*   **200-second chart:** Used specifically for entries. "The 200 second breaks down that 10-minute chart into three equal parts. Okay. So you know how you would have a 15-minute chart and a 5-minute? Yeah. Same concept."

### 2. Exact Entry Setup
The core of his strategy relies on the last two digits of the Nasdaq price level ending in 80 or 20 (e.g., 25680 or 25620). He waits for price to reach these levels and looks for specific reactions on the 200-second chart. He outlines three specific setups:

**Setup A: The Fork (Mean Reversion)**
1.  A strong directional move occurs.
2.  A candle prints with a long wick and a short body that *fails* to touch the 80 or 20 level.
3.  The very next candle tests the extreme (high/low) of the previous long wick but fails to break it, and then reverses direction.
4.  **Entry:** "your entry would be this candle here [the second candle]... you're looking for this candle to test this low right here but not break it and then end up looking something like this... making a higher high... you couldn't break this low... this is where you would take an entry"

**Setup B: The Cross Section (Continuation/Reversal)**
1.  Two strong, large-bodied candles print consecutively in the same direction.
2.  This leaves a "cross section" (a gap or imbalance) between the close of the first and the open of the second.
3.  Price retraces back into this cross section, ideally aligning with an 80 or 20 level.
4.  **Entry:** "you have these two strong candles higher and you have that cross section between them... It's also a 20 level... your entry would be at this cross section and 20."

**Setup C: The 'h' Pattern (Continuation)**
1.  A strong move down, followed by a brief bounce (forming the left side and peak of a lowercase 'h').
2.  Price rolls over to continue the downtrend, often rejecting off a cross section or an 80/20 level.
3.  **Entry:** Enter short on the rollover to target previous lows or "repair" candles below. (A "repair candle" is a strong candle with no wick on one side, which acts as a magnet for price to return to).

### 3. Stop Placement Rule
He uses a strict, static point-based stop loss on every trade.
> "I always use a 10 point stop. Always. That's how I keep my risk under control because if I'm wrong by 10, most likely I'm probably wrong by 50 or 100."

### 4. Target Rule and Trade Management
He scales out of his positions to secure risk quickly, then leaves runners.
*   **Target 1 (Risk Coverage):** "The first initial one, yeah, it's going to be a 15 point take profit just so I cover my risk."
*   **Trade Management:** "Once you take that first TP, is that when you move to break even? Yeah."
*   **Runners:** After moving to break even, he lets the remaining contracts run toward the next logical level (like the next 80/20 level or a "repair" magnet candle), noting that on capitulation moves, "you can squeeze, you know, 40, 50, 60 points."

### 5. Risk per Trade or Sizing Rule
He does not change his stop loss distance to adjust risk; instead, he changes his position size (number of contracts).
> "instead of a point value, my point value stays static, I would just put on more contracts."

If trading 3 contracts, he takes 2 off at the 15-point target and leaves 1 runner. If trading 4 contracts, he takes 2 off initially, then 1 at the next level, leaving 1 runner.

### 6. Filters & Avoidance Rules
*   He strictly avoids trading during the lunch hour.
*   He avoids "slow, choppy conditions or slow trending conditions," preferring the high volatility of the NY Open.

### 7. Discretionary / Feel-Based Elements ⚠️
While the levels and stops are rigid, trade frequency and holding runners involve discretion based on market "feel."
*   **Discretion on Runners:** "that's where you kind of have to make your own judgment call... you get to grow into the instrument and kind of learn exactly when you are right and when you are wrong... based on again that feeling and sort of how you're doing on that day."
*   **Discretion on Frequency:** "if you're hot, step on the gas... if the market is hot and the model gives you so many entries... step on the gas, make your money." Conversely, if the market is choppy, he advises stepping away.

### 8. Specific Live Trades Shown
The video includes a heavily edited live trading segment showing him trading on his phone (dates appear to be around June 24-26, 2024).
*   **Trade 1 (Short):** He identifies a "repair" level acting as a magnet around 24848. He places a Sell Limit order and gets filled short. He takes his first partial profit (15 points) and states, "At this point we can move our stop to break even." The market reverses, and he is stopped out on the remaining contracts at break even.
*   **Trade 2 (Long):** Price drops rapidly toward an "80" level (around 24780). He enters long manually ("on the bid") near 24780. He targets the "20" level above (24820). He takes his first partial profit as it moves up, moves his stop to break even, and is subsequently stopped out on the runners.
