# Kris Verma's Small-Cap Shorting Strategy: Structured Rule Sheet

## 1. Instrument(s) and Session/Time Windows

Kris Verma trades exclusively small-cap stocks that gap up significantly in the pre-market (typically 100%+ moves). He avoids holding positions overnight due to fees and risk, stating, "I don't try to hold overnights."

His primary time windows are highly specific and tied to market psychology and liquidity conditions:

**Pre-Market Window (4:00 AM - 6:00 AM ET):** This is the optimal entry window for "front-side shorts" (early entries while the stock is still pushing). He states, "From 4:00 AM to 6:00 AM is the ideal time to get these kinds of moves because you have the most time to allow it to fade."

**Market Open (9:30 AM ET):** The primary action area where most volume concentrates.

**10:00 AM - 11:00 AM Check-in:** A critical time window for assessing whether a trade is working. He states, "If a stock is above the open price after 10:00 AM, to me that's a red flag... After 10:00 AM or 11:00 AM if a pattern is failing then I'm looking to downsize my risk on the short side, limit my exposure and even flipping long."

**30-Minute Rule:** A critical early assessment point. He states, "If after 30 minutes I'm in the green, then I'm going to continue holding. If after 30 minutes I'm not in the green and it's reclaiming on me, then I'm reassessing what to do."

## 2. Exact Entry Setup

Verma describes a shift from a purely systematic "short the open" approach to a more patient "backside" approach due to changing market conditions. The backside approach waits for the stock to attempt a push and fail, creating a topping pattern, before entering.

**Front-Side Short (Earlier Approach - Less Preferred Now):**
He describes this as entering while the stock is still actively pushing up or halting. "This was a pattern that I traded earlier in the year and is more so of a front-side short... A lot of short sellers will short this halt resume and that's what we used to do."

**Backside Short (Current Preferred Approach):**
The exact entry sequence is:

1. **Stock Gaps Up Significantly:** The stock gaps up 100%+ in the pre-market.
2. **Topping Action Develops:** The stock attempts to push higher but gets rejected, leaving a visible wick on top, indicating a lack of buying strength.
3. **Volume Declines:** As the stock forms the top, volume must be declining, confirming that manipulation and buying interest are fading.
4. **Pullback and Clear Out:** The stock pulls back and then clears out (breaks through) the previous highs one more time.
5. **Entry on Downside Candle:** Enter short on the downside candle after the failed breakout.

His exact quote on the complete setup:
> "Instead of hitting that first candle, right, I'm looking for the pullback and then the clear out of the previous highs... I'm looking to wait for a bit of a topping candle, so this wick right here kind of confirms that it's failing. And then you'll also have volume profile that's declining as this comes down. Right? And then you want to add on the way down to maximize your return once it's confirmed... hitting the downside candle and then catching a clean fade."

**The ADF (All Day Fade) Setup:** By 10:00 AM, the stock must be below the open price by at least 15%, and the volume profile must be decreasing.

**Topping Candle/Wick Definition:** A candle that attempts to push higher but gets rejected, leaving a visible wick on top. He states, "So instead of trying to guess where the top is, we're just waiting for, you know, some topping action, a bit of a wick, and then hitting the downside candle and then catching a clean fade."

## 3. Stop Placement Rule

Verma explicitly advises against using the pre-market high as a stop loss, as it is a common liquidity trap where market makers will clear the highs to stop out traders before the stock dumps.

Instead, he uses a **fixed percentage stop that is unusually wide** to avoid manipulation:
> "Using the pre-market high is not a good stop. Because a lot of the times they'll look to clear the highs, creating a liquidity event, stopping people out, and then the stock proceeds to dump immediately... So what I use now is a fixed percentage stop. Let's say, you know, a wide stop 75 to 150% from the entry. Which sounds a bit crazy, but that's what the back test show yields the most returns."

He does not specify which exact percentage (75% or 150%) he uses more frequently, but rather provides it as a general range for his fixed percentage stop approach.

## 4. Target Rule and Trade Management

Verma manages trades actively by taking partial profits at support levels and "recycling" (re-shorting) on bounces. This approach allows him to capture multiple moves within a single stock's trading range while reducing borrowing fees.

**Taking Profits at Support Levels:**
He identifies prior pre-market support and resistance areas, as well as specific moving averages on certain timeframes. He states, "And then it comes back down to this prior pre-market support area. Right? So nowadays I would look to take profits here... Because it is at a prior support level and it's likely to bounce. So why not take, you know, half the position off right here."

**The 10:00 AM Rule (Exit Trigger):**
If the stock is above the open price after 10:00 AM, he downsizes or exits. "If a stock is above the open price after 10:00 AM, to me that's a red flag... After 10:00 AM or 11:00 AM if a pattern is failing then I'm looking to downsize my risk on the short side, limit my exposure and even flipping long."

**The 30-Minute Rule (Early Assessment):**
> "If after 30 minutes I'm in the green, then I'm going to continue holding. If after 30 minutes I'm not in the green and it's reclaiming on me, then I'm reassessing what to do. Right? I might be downsizing into dips, I might be cutting the position altogether, or I might lower my stop to the prior highs."

**Recycling Strategy:**
After taking profits, he re-enters on the bounce at prior resistance levels. "And then look to recycle at this prior resistance area. Because once it cracks this level, support becomes resistance. So you can reshort here..." This approach allows him to capture multiple smaller moves rather than betting on a single directional fade.

He explains the mechanics of recycling: "So the recycling is one of the biggest approaches that I've taken this year... Just trying to identify the range that it's playing and make more money off of the moves within that range versus looking for a one-directional move... And you can recycle the shares. And like I said before, this helps reduce your costs. Because you're not having to locate so many shares. So you can locate, you know, a certain amount of shares, then you take some off, then you add them back... So most stocks will give you those locates for the whole day... So you can just recycle them over and over instead of having to pay, you know, excessive fees building on a position."

**Moving Average Targets:**
He uses specific EMAs on certain timeframes as target levels. "You can see, you know, certain ranges that they're trading, trend lines... I use moving averages, EMAs. I feel like the three-minute chart and the 15-minute chart are the most respected among the EMAs. The stocks like to respect those charts. So I use the 9 and the 20 EMA on those timeframes."

## 5. Risk Per Trade or Sizing Rule

Verma does not state a specific percentage of his account risked per trade. However, his sizing is dynamic based on the confirmation level of the pattern:

**Initial Entry Sizing:** He sizes smaller on initial entries or if a pattern is unconfirmed.

**Confirmed Entry Sizing:** He sizes heavier once the "backside" is confirmed (i.e., after the stock has failed to hold the highs and is clearly topping). He states, "So these here [after the pattern fails to hold highs], I'd be sizing in a little heavier once it's failed because the probability of it reclaiming is a lot lower."

The exact sizing multiplier is not specified, but the principle is clear: **smaller size on unconfirmed entries, larger size on confirmed patterns.**

## 6. Filters & Avoidance Rules

Verma applies several strict filters to avoid low-probability or overly risky trades:

**Market Cap Filter:**
> "The market cap is probably the most important thing for me. I don't want to trade anything over like 200 million market cap. Because it just kind of confirms the legitimacy of the company. If it's over 200 million it's not, you know, really a shitco as they call them."

**Institutional Ownership Filter:**
> "I don't like institutional percentage over 40%."

**Volume Filter:**
He requires decreasing volume to confirm a fade pattern. High volume indicates manipulation and buying interest. He states, "You want to see the volume decreasing. Okay. Cause that kind of indicates less interest in the stock, less kind of manipulation. Whenever there's high volume it kind of favors more manipulation and more buying interest."

He further emphasizes: "And then I also want to see where's the volume. Right? If the volume's declining on this kind of pattern, then there's a little bit more confirmation that this is the backside, the top is in."

**Pre-Market Volume Context:**
He notes that pre-market volume is intentionally low to make it easier and cheaper to manipulate the stock price upwards. "And there's a reason they do this in pre-market is because it requires less volume to pump the stock. So for the riggers it requires less money for them to create this move."

## 7. Discretionary / Feel-Based Elements ⚠️

Verma explicitly states his approach is a hybrid: **"I am using probably let's say 70% system, 30% discretion."**

The discretion is applied primarily in trade management within the first 30 minutes—reading the tape, assessing volume, and deciding whether to cut a trade early, recycle shares, or hold based on how the price action "feels" compared to his historical data.

Specific discretionary elements include:

*   **Early Exit Decision (30-Minute Window):** Whether to cut the position, downsize, or hold based on how the stock is performing relative to expectations.
*   **Recycling Timing:** Deciding exactly when to take profits and re-enter based on support/resistance levels and tape reading.
*   **Stop Adjustment:** Lowering his stop to prior highs if the pattern is failing early.
